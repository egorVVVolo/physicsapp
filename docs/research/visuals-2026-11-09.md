# Proposal: Visual direction, demonstration toolkit and build method for Physicsapp (WPH11)

*Labels: **[F]** = sourced fact, **[O]** = observed but not documented, **[R]** = recommendation, **[I]** = inference that still needs testing. Items marked **Owner decides** change something already agreed.*

---

## 1. Why the previous visuals failed

1. **The motion was not the physics.** Nose dip, wheel spin and smoke drew the eye and carried no information. **[F]** Learners look at whatever moves, and novices are affected most [8]. Seductive details hurt transfer. The harm is worst when the details are visual and time is short [9], and a 10-minute session is short.
2. **The follow camera hid displacement.** The s–t graph described something the eye could not see. **[F]** A graphic's form should match the concept it shows [7].
3. **Graph easing contradicted the model.** **[R]** Nothing physical gets UI easing.
4. **Realism and a dark "premium" theme.** **[F]** Animations should "lean toward the schematic and away from the realistic" [7]. **[O]** Brilliant's homepage lesson cards use thin strokes on off-white, with 1–2 flat accents and no textures or 3D [5].
5. **Watching, not predicting.** **[F]** Predicting before a demo helps modestly: fully correct explanations rose from 24% to 30%. Discussing the result helped more [11]. So the toolkit pairs each prediction with a "why" step.
6. **Easy questions, with the answer visible before the learner committed.** That is how you get "a 5th grader could answer this".
7. **Text-heavy screens with small static diagrams.**

**Diagnosis:** the old work polished the wrong layer. Polish should come from clarity, from direct manipulation and from exact sync, not from effects [1][3].

## 2. Visual language: "the living exam diagram"

1. **Flat schematic bodies only.** Blocks are rounded rectangles with the mass inside ("2.0 kg"). Balls are circles with a centre-of-mass dot. Pulleys are a circle plus an axle dot. Strings are 1.5 px lines. Fixed surfaces get hatching. Nothing has gradients or shadows [7][17]. **There are no real-object drawings.** Context goes in one line of text ("a lift in a 40-storey tower").
2. **Fixed world frame with a ruler,** ticked every 1 m. There is never a follow camera. If the motion is wide, the view zooms out.
3. **Checked-in `src/theme/tokens.ts`** holds every value below. Prototypes and the app share it.
   - **Palette: 5 colours at most [I].** Ink `#1B1D22` (structure, text). Velocity `#0B5FCC` blue. Acceleration `#B45309` amber. Force `#A3123A` crimson; all forces share it and the label says which force. Event/cursor `#00796B` teal. Background `#F6F4EF`. A script checks every token: text ≥ 4.5:1, arrows ≥ 3:1, and pairs are told apart under deuteranopia and protanopia simulation. Any token that fails is replaced before M1.
   - **Meaning never comes from colour alone.** Force arrows have a filled head. Velocity arrows have an open chevron. Acceleration arrows have a double chevron and sit beside the body. The resultant is dashed and labelled ΣF. The learner's ghost is a grey dashed outline.
   - **Type:** Inter (bundled). Scale 13 / 15 / 17 / 22 px. Labels are 13 px semibold.
   - **Scale rules:** px-per-metre is chosen so the whole motion fits at ≥ 90% of the scene width. Each scene has one N-per-px or (m/s)-per-px scale, shown by a key. Arrows are at least 12 px long. Arrows longer than the clamp are drawn with a break marker.
   - **Label placement:** labels sit on the arrow's outer side, 6 px off the tip, and flip side if they would collide.
   - **Strokes:** 2 px for structure, 1 px for guides.
4. **Arrows are data.** Each arrow starts at its point of application and carries a symbol, a value and a unit ("T = 14.7 N"). The mark scheme gives a mark per labelled, correctly placed arrow, and examiners flag misplaced reaction forces [17]. A `ContactPoint` primitive offsets N and friction at the same face by fixed distances so they never overlap.
5. **Layering (progressive reveal).** Only one new layer appears per screen. The order is dots, then graph, then arrows. A ghost comparison shows at most the scene plus **one** graph. PhET starts every readout off for the same reason [13].
6. **Text and layout:** at most two lines of text. The scene takes at least 60% of the height **[I]**, to be checked on a phone.
7. **Motion rules:**
   - Physical motion runs on linear model time.
   - Nothing moves until the learner acts.
   - Playback **auto-pauses at events** (v = 0, apex, impact, divergence), with one cue line ("watch the spacing") before a replay. Novices rarely use the controls themselves [7].
   - Controls are pause, scrub, 0.25× and step.
   - Reduced motion jumps between key frames.
   - Celebrations last at most 400 ms and never cover the scene.
8. **"Great animation", defined as a polish budget that carries information:**
   - scrubbing follows the finger with less than 1 frame of lag
   - the graph trace is drawn in sync and pixel-aligned to the time cursor
   - a soft haptic tick fires at physical events
   - text is crisp
   - nothing shifts in the layout

## 3. Demonstration toolkit

### Shared scene-spec rules

These apply to every pattern.

- Each element declares `reveal: "always" | "afterCheck"`. The validator fails if any element bound to the answer quantity is `"always"`. A test renders at t = 0 and t = T before Check and asserts that no `afterCheck` element is present.
- Every handle has a randomised range, and its snap step is set so the answer is neither the midpoint nor a snap point.
- On a question screen, **scrub is locked until Check**.
- Each scene declares `ghostSource: "signs" | "sketch" | "forces" | "none"`. A ghost appears **only** when the learner's input defines a motion.
- For single-number answers (`"none"`): the real body freezes at the learner's value, and a model-computed label states the gap. Example: "at your t = 2.5 s the ball is 4.1 m above the ground".
- Each scene declares a `tolerance`, computed from the model (for example ±2% for numeric answers).
- Every ghost replay lasts at most 5 s. A one-tap "why?" question follows, with options that name the misconceptions [11][12].

### Kinematics MVP (3 patterns)

**★ 1. Ghost Run.**
- *On screen:* the scene, and one input that defines a motion: the sign and value of u, the sign of g, or a chosen SUVAT equation.
- *Learner:* sets the input and the answer, then taps Check.
- *Moves:* the real motion and the dashed ghost run on one clock. Both auto-freeze at the divergence point. A bracket then labels the gap ("Δs = 3.2 m").
- *Example (a trap from the list in §5):* a ball is released from a balloon rising at 4 m/s, 30 m up. The learner picks the ball's initial velocity (0, 4 m/s up, 4 m/s down) and the time to hit the ground. A learner who picks "u = 0" sees their ghost fall straight away while the real ball first rises. All values come from `src/physics/`.

**2. Graph ↔ World Scrub.**
- *On screen:* the scene above a v–t graph that shares its time axis.
- *Learner, after Check:* touches the graph. The scene jumps to that moment, a tangent segment shows the slope, and the area shades up to that time [14].
- *Breaks:* slope vs height, and reading the graph as a picture of the path [15].
- *Projectiles, first screen:* the path, x–t and y–t shown side by side, with the question "Which of these is the path?"

**3. Strobe Ruler.**
- *On screen:* dots dropped every 0.2 s on the ruler [34].
- *Learner:* places the next 3 dots.
- *Graded:* the spacing ratios must be within ±15% of the model's. The real dots are drawn **over** the learner's, which stay visible.
- *Example:* a car braking at 4 m/s².

### Forces module (later)

**4. Force Builder → Release.**
- *On screen:* a block **sliding up** a rough 30° slope.
- *Learner:* drags arrows out of allowed attachment points (`ContactPoint`, centre of mass), labels them and sets their lengths.
- *Moves:* the ghost moves **only along the slope**, using `constrainedAccel(F_learner · ŝ, m)`. Any leftover perpendicular part is shown as a dashed arrow ("unbalanced 4.9 N ⟂ slope"). The ghost never sinks or lifts off.
- *FBD check rules:*
  - every force starts at an allowed point, with the right direction and label, and lengths within ±20% of the model ratio
  - kinetic friction opposes the velocity
  - static friction opposes the **impending** motion
- *Breaks:* "a force in the direction of motion", and N = mg on a slope.

**5. Split FBD (replaces the Constraint Pulse).**
- *On screen:* the connected system and two free-body diagrams side by side, each with its own F = ma.
- *Learner:* predicts T.
- *Moves:* two ghosts run on the predicted T. The table block accelerates at a = T/m₁, and the hanging block at g − T/m₂. If T = m₂g, the hanging block stays still while the table block speeds up, and the string visibly breaks the constraint.
- *Supporting pieces:* `StringPath` (a polyline with pulley tangents) for routing over slope edges or a second pulley.

**6. Component Lens.**
- *Learner:* chooses the axes and drags θ to 0° and 90° as a sanity check.
- *Moves:* the components grow out of the parent arrow, which fades to 30%.
- *Breaks:* swapping sin and cos. **[F]** Algodoo cannot resolve along a slope [35], so this fills that gap.

**7. Twin Worlds.** Two cases on one clock that differ in one variable. The learner bets on the outcome before they run [16].

### After the pilot

**8. Sketch-the-Graph.** The learner draws a v–t curve and the sketch drives a ghost. Grading checks the sign and slope region by region, not exact points.

**Static cases** (equilibrium, moments) use a vector triangle or a resolving interaction, never a fake animation.

## 4. Animation stack decision

### Verified version facts [F]

- The latest stable Expo SDK is **58** (15 Oct 2026), with React Native 0.88 and React 19.3. Expo Go for SDK 58 is due in the stores "within a few weeks". Until then, `create-expo-app` creates **SDK 57** projects. Once the new store Expo Go arrives, it runs **only** SDK 58 [18].
- App Store Expo Go has run SDK 57 since 3 Sep 2026. It needs the same Expo account in the CLI (`npx expo login`) and in the app [19]. Expo Go for SDK 55 waited months for Apple approval [21].
- **SDK 57 ships with** Reanimated 4.5, worklets 0.10 and Gesture Handler 2.32 [20].
- **SDK 58 recommends** Reanimated 4.7.1, Gesture Handler ~3.3, react-native-svg 15.16.0 and Skia 2.14.0 [22][23][24][25].
- Gesture Handler 3 adds hook-style gestures, and the builder and hook styles cannot be mixed [26].
- Reanimated, Gesture Handler, SVG and Skia all run in Expo Go. Skia on web needs a CanvasKit WebAssembly download [25]. **Rive needs a development build** [28].
- Lottie's Expo Go support is documented only for SDK 50 [37], so it is unverified for 57/58. It can only play pre-made clips anyway.
- Reanimated's docs suggest about 100 animated components on low-end Android, and mention Skia for more [27]. Expo Go behaves like a debug build, which makes it slower [27]. Expo's benchmark was written by the maker of a competing library [38].

### Decision [R]: react-native-svg + Reanimated + Gesture Handler

This keeps the agreed plan. Skia is used only if the stress test fails.

- **Why SVG is enough:** a strobe trail, a graph curve and a ghost path are each **one `Path` with one animated `d`**. A full scene then has about 15–25 animated props, far below about 100. Text, web, hit areas and accessibility work natively. Agents also have more SVG training data.
- **One renderer, no abstraction layer.** Scene files never mention the renderer. If a switch is ever needed, about 10 primitives get rewritten.
- **Owner decides:** Skia replaces SVG only if the stress scene misses its target.
- **Physics engines:** none. Numerical integration drifts from the SUVAT answer key.

| Job | Tool |
|---|---|
| Scene drawing | react-native-svg, one `<Svg>` per scene |
| Clock `t` and everything derived from it | Reanimated shared values and `useDerivedValue` |
| Gestures | Hook module `src/scene/gestures.ts` (`useDragHandle`, `useScrub`, `useSketch`). Only this file changes for Gesture Handler v3 |
| Text, buttons, inputs | Ordinary React Native views |
| Physics | `src/physics/`, pure and tested |
| Later UI flourishes | Possibly Rive, after the accounts milestone |

### Versions

- At the start of M1, check which SDK the store Expo Go runs, because 58 may already be out.
- If it is 57: turn off auto-update for Expo Go (iPhone: Settings → App Store → App Updates off, or keep 57 until the upgrade is done). Upgrade to 58 the week Expo Go 58 appears, with `npx expo install expo@^58 --fix`.
- The owner gets numbered login steps for both the CLI and the app.

### How the agent builds a scene

This follows the Manim / Motion Canvas idea of one tracker driving everything [29].

1. **Model, returning phases and events.** `simulate(params) → { phases: [{t0, t1, s(t), v(t), a(t), forces}], events: [{t, kind: "apex"|"impact"|"slack"|"static"|"stop"}] }`. There is one generalised coordinate s(t), and every body's position is a declared function of s (for example, the hanging block = −s). The model covers slack strings, a block that stops and stays put (tan θ < μ), and friction that changes direction. The renderer auto-pauses at events.
2. **Scene file, data only:** world size in metres, bodies, arrow → vector bindings, graph → quantity, handle → parameter (range, snap step, units), `reveal`, `ghostSource`, `tolerance`. The agent writes these files. **[F]** Brilliant's generated-puzzle success went from 0% to 93% by making the representation LLM-friendly, not by switching models [1].
3. **MVP primitives:** Body, Ruler, Arrow, Graph, TimeCursor, Strobe, Ghost, Handle, ScrubBar. `Surface`, `StringPath`, `Pulley`, `ContactPoint` and `FBDBuilder` come with Forces. Animation uses a plain `play(t0, t1)`; there is no choreography timeline until a scene needs one.
4. **Verification:**
   - unit tests check that arrows equal the model, the cursor matches the body, and the reveal test passes
   - Brilliant-style evals [2]: no bounding-box overlaps, every arrow starts at an allowed point, every label has units
   - state-JSON snapshots at t = 0, T/2 and T
   - a web route `?scene=balloon&t=1.2&paused=1` for exact screenshots
   - there are no golden PNGs
   - finally, a check on the owner's phone; passing tests does not mean the design is approved
5. **Stress scene:** ghost + 1 graph + 6 arrows + 60 dots, as batched paths. It is measured in Expo Go first. Only if it falls below about 50 fps does the agent run one local `npx expo run:ios --configuration Release`, with step-by-step instructions for the owner.

## 5. Session structure

### Core session (about 8 screens, measured on the first prototype) [R]

| # | Screen |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Qualitative hook:** "before or after 3 s?" or "which hits first?". Not counted toward the success target [36] |
| 2 | Replay with auto-pauses, plus one line that names the idea |
| 3 | Faded worked example: steps 1–2 shown, learner fills in step 3 [33]. **Skipped if the hook was right;** comes back after 2 misses |
| 4–6 | Three practice items at R2–R3, at least one of them R4, with traps from the topic's list |
| 7 | Honest summary: independent / assisted / revealed per item |
| 8 | Next puzzle as a frozen frame **[I]**: the cliffhanger effect is weakly evidenced |

On a wrong answer: Ghost Run or the frozen-at-your-t view, then "why?", one graded hint, and one retry. **[I]** Target 70–85% first-try success on screens 3–6.

**Required trap list per topic (the content must cover all of them):**
- a ball released from a rising balloon
- total distance vs displacement when v–t crosses the axis
- two bodies meeting
- a projectile landing on a raised ledge
- a block that stops on a rough slope and stays (tan θ < μ)
- a hanging mass that hits the floor, after which the string goes slack and the second phase decelerates
- a pulley on a rough incline where friction changes direction

### Depth sessions [R]

- **Tricky cases** (+1–3 days): no worked examples, R4, Legendary-style [31].
- **Exam workout** (+about 1 week): interleaved across 2–4 topics, original exam-format items with "(3)" marks, no hints, then a review in mark-scheme style. Khan Academy reserves "Mastered" for tests like this [32]. **[F]** Testing beats re-study after a delay [30].
- **Spaced retrieval:** one item from an earlier topic, here rather than in Core.
- **Mistakes review:** 2–4 weeks later, and before mocks.

## 6. Cheap validation before writing lots of code

**[R]** **Round 1 (2 sessions):** two phone-sized HTML/SVG pages built on `tokens.ts`, both using the **balloon question**. They differ **only in layout**:
- **A. Scene-first:** the scene takes 60% or more of the screen, with the graph strip below.
- **B. Graph-first:** a large v–t graph, with a thin scene strip below.

The owner scores each from 1 to 5 on four questions:
- Do I understand it in 3 s without text?
- Did I want to predict?
- Did the ghost teach me something?
- Does it look good, or does it make me cringe?

The prototype also measures the real session time.

**Round 2:** compare interaction types (Ghost vs frozen-at-your-t, Strobe vs none) in the winning layout.

**In parallel:** the SVG stress scene in Expo Go. M1 primitives start only after both.

## Rejected critique points

- **Keep the real-object hook in prototype C as a hypothesis (critique 3).** Rejected. The owner has already called clip-art-style visuals childish, so critique 1's full removal wins. Context goes in one line of text.
- **Unconstrained `constantAccel2D` ghost for Force Builder (critique 2).** Partly rejected. A free 2D ghost produces the "sinks into the slope" cartoon. The ghost stays analytic but moves only along the constraint, and any perpendicular residual is shown as a labelled arrow.
- **Interleaving RCT as support for one old item per Core.** Accepted in substance: the item moved to Depth and is relabelled spaced retrieval.
